Primer for newcomers · humanoid series, part 2

Puppeteer: a world model that pulls the strings

A second paper explained the same way: diagrams first, every equation with a worked number. It reuses the basics from the SuperMimic primer (the RL loop, rewards, PD control), so read that one first if those are new.

The paper

Two world-model agents stacked. A low-level tracker learns once to make a 56-joint simulated humanoid follow 5 target points (head, hands, feet) taken from human motion. A high-level puppeteer looks at a 64×64 camera image and decides where those 5 points go next. It earns about the same reward as a flat baseline, but people rated its motion more natural 97.8% of the time.

Its world model

TD-MPC2: squeeze the state into a short vector \(z\), predict the next \(z\), the reward and the value, and never rebuild the image. Actions come from planning: try 512 candidate plans inside the model at every step and keep the best.

The lesson

Split “how to move the body” from “where to go”. One body skill, learned from human motion, is reused for every task and keeps the motion human-like without hand-tuned style rewards.

Part 1 · Background

Three ideas you need for this paper

Idea 1 · Hierarchy: one agent decides, another moves

Controlling 56 joints straight from pixels is too hard to learn from task reward alone. So the problem is split. The puppeteer only says where the head, hands and feet should go. The tracker works out the joint commands that get them there.

Two policies, chained
\[ c_t = \pi^{\text{hi}}(q_t,\, v_t) \qquad\qquad a_t = \pi^{\text{lo}}(q_t,\, c_t) \]
  • \(q_t\) body state212 numbers: joint angles and speeds, velocity, acceleration, gyro, joint torques, touch sensors, uprightness
  • \(v_t\) camera image64 × 64 × 3 = 12,288 numbers from a third-person camera
  • \(c_t\) command15 numbers: where head, two hands and two feet should be next
  • \(a_t\) action56 joint commands, each scaled to \([-1, 1]\)
The puppeteer picks 15 numbers instead of 56. A command like “right foot 0.3 m further forward” is enough; the tracker works out how hip, knee and ankle must move to make it happen.

Idea 2 · A latent world model that never draws pictures

Both agents use the same world-model design, TD-MPC2. It encodes the state into a latent vector \(z\) (a short learned summary, 512 numbers here) and predicts everything in that space. It never tries to reconstruct the image. This style is called JEPA-like or decoder-free.

The six components · paper Eq. 1
\[ z=h(s)\qquad z'=d(z,a)\qquad \hat r=R(z,a)\qquad \hat\delta=D(z,a)\qquad \hat q=Q(z,a)\qquad \hat a=p(z) \]

\(\hat\delta\) is the predicted chance the episode ends here. \(\hat q\) is the predicted discounted future reward. \(\hat a\) is a quick first guess at a good action.

How it is trained · TD-MPC2's loss, with this paper's weights
\[ \mathcal L_{\text{TD-MPC2}}=\mathbb E\sum_{t=0}^{H}\lambda^t\Big(\underbrace{20\,\big\lVert z'_{t+1}-\operatorname{sg}\big(h(s_{t+1})\big)\big\rVert^2}_{\text{predict next latent}}+\underbrace{0.1\,\mathrm{CE}(\hat r_t,r_t)}_{\text{reward}}+\underbrace{0.1\,\mathrm{CE}(\hat q_t,q_t)}_{\text{value}}\Big) \] \[ q_t = r_t + \gamma\,\bar Q\big(z'_{t+1},\,p(z'_{t+1})\big), \qquad \gamma = 0.97 \]

sg = stop-gradient: the target is treated as a constant, so the encoder cannot cheat by making everything identical. CE = cross-entropy (TD-MPC2 predicts reward and value as classes over bins). \(\bar Q\) = a slowly updated copy of \(Q\). \(H = 3\), \(\lambda = 0.5\).

\(\lambda = 0.5\) weights the 4 prediction steps by 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125. Getting the next step right matters most; errors 3 steps out still count, but less.
Puppeteer's addition · paper Eq. 2
\[ \mathcal L_{\text{Puppeteer}} = \mathcal L_{\text{TD-MPC2}} + \alpha\,\mathrm{CE}(\hat\delta,\,\delta), \qquad \alpha = 0.1 \]

\(\delta = 1\) if a body part other than the feet touched the floor (a fall), else 0. Without this, the baseline learned bizarre crawling and rolling.

Idea 3 · Planning: try many futures, act on the best

TD-MPC2 does not just run a policy network. At every control step it plans with MPPI (model predictive path integral), a sampling search: guess many short action sequences, score them in the world model, move the guess toward the best ones, repeat, then do only the first action.

Score of one plan · with soft termination
\[ J(a_{0:H-1})=\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\gamma^h\,w_h\,\hat r_h+\gamma^H\,w_H\,\hat q_H, \qquad w_h=\max\Big(0,\;1-\sum_{iRoll the plan through \(d\), add up predicted rewards, and let the value head \(\hat q\) stand in for everything after step \(H\). The weight \(w_h\) shrinks rewards that come after a likely fall.

The paper says it keeps a running sum of termination chances, capped, as a soft cut-off; the formula for \(w_h\) is how we read that. If the model predicts \(\hat\delta = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3\), the weights are 1, 0.9, 0.7, and the final value gets 0.4.
Refit step · over the 64 elites
\[ \Omega_i = e^{\tau\,(J_i - \max_j J_j)}, \qquad \mu = \frac{\sum_i \Omega_i\, a^{(i)}}{\sum_i \Omega_i}, \qquad \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_i \Omega_i\,\big(a^{(i)}-\mu\big)^2}{\sum_i \Omega_i}}, \qquad \tau = 0.5 \]
Two elites score \(J = 10\) and \(J = 8\). Weights: \(e^{0} = 1\) and \(e^{-1} = 0.37\). The new mean is 73% plan 1 and 27% plan 2, and the spread shrinks to cover just these two.
SuperMimic used its world model during training only: it backpropagated through 32 imagined steps, then the policy acted alone in 1 forward pass. TD-MPC2 searches inside its model every time it acts. Slower per step, but it can fix a mediocre policy on the fly.

Puppeteer: train the body once, then each task

Stage 1 · The tracker

  • Data. MoCapAct: 836 human motion clips (CMU mocap, retargeted to the 56-joint “CMU Humanoid”), 20 noisy rollouts each = 16,720 trajectories, all labelled with a tracking reward.
  • Command. Where the reference's head, hands and feet are at the next step, relative to the body: \(c_t\), 15 numbers.
  • Mixing. Each batch is half old logs, half the agent's own fresh play. Offline logs alone failed badly (see Fig 2.2).

Stage 2 · The puppeteer and the tasks

Eight tasks on the 56-joint CMU Humanoid in DMControl (a MuJoCo-based simulator suite). Five use the camera; three use body state only. An episode ends after 500 steps or when any non-foot body part touches the floor.

Camera + body state

Reward for the 5 camera tasks · paper Eq. 3
\[ R(s)=\operatorname{clip}\big(v_x,\;0,\;v_{\text{target}}\big), \qquad v_{\text{target}} = 6\ \text{m/s} \]
Reward for stand, walk, run · paper Eq. 4
\[ R(s)=\min\big(\lvert v_{xy}\rvert,\;v_{\text{target}}\big)+\alpha\cdot h_{\text{head}} \]

\(v_x\) = forward speed, \(v_{xy}\) = speed in any horizontal direction, \(h_{\text{head}}\) = head height. Nothing in these rewards says “move like a human”.

Running forward at 2 m/s earns 2 per step. Backing up earns 0, not a penalty. Sprinting at 8 m/s is capped at 6.

Lessons for your own humanoid work

  1. Watch the robot, not just the return. Rolling up stairs scored better than walking. Always look at rollouts, and measure something about style (torso height, falls) next to the reward.
  2. Use human motion as the style prior. Instead of writing rewards for “be graceful”, learn a tracker from mocap and let task reward act only through it.
  3. Pick a readable interface between levels. 5 body points in 3D is small, easy to inspect, and works for any task. Learned embeddings are harder to debug.
  4. Mix old data with fresh play. Offline logs alone: 6.2% of clips tracked. Half and half: 88.3%.
  5. Model falls explicitly. A termination head, softened during planning, kept the baseline from degenerate crawling.
  6. In high dimensions, planning pays. Planning at both levels: 43.0. Policy networks alone: 1.6.

For the general checklist (dynamics, frames, domain randomization, hardware safety) see Part 4 of the SuperMimic primer.

Reference

Glossary

Hierarchical RL
splitting control into a high level that sets goals and a low level that reaches them.
End effector
a body part at the end of a chain: head, hands, feet.
Proprioception
the body's own sensors: joint angles, speeds, IMU, touch.
Latent state \(z\)
a learned compact vector that summarizes the state.
Decoder-free / JEPA-style
a world model that predicts future latents, never pixels.
MPC
model predictive control: plan a short sequence, do the first action, replan.
MPPI
a sampling-based MPC planner: sample, score, re-weight toward the best, repeat.
Elites
the top-scoring candidate plans (64 here) used to refit the sampler.
Policy prior
a small policy network whose guesses seed the planner.
TD target
reward now plus discounted predicted value next; the training target for \(Q\).
Stop-gradient (sg)
treat a value as a constant during backprop.
Termination
the episode ending, here when a non-foot body part hits the floor.
MoCapAct
a public dataset of rollouts from policies that track CMU mocap clips.
DMControl
DeepMind Control Suite: MuJoCo-based simulated control tasks.
Reward hacking
scoring high in a way the reward designer did not intend.
